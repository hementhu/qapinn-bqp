\begin{abstract}
This is the extended working version of the QAPINN-BQP report. It contains the
full technical content of the submission draft, an \emph{adversarial self-audit}
that corrected our initial over-claims, the \emph{converged re-analysis} that the
audit forced, and an explicit account of everything still \emph{left out} of scope.
The narrative arc is honest and worth stating plainly. An initial single-seed
ablation suggested a ``bandwidth-gated advantage''; the audit then showed those
runs were \emph{undertrained} (L-BFGS disabled; every Burgers model stuck at
rel-$L_2\approx0.47$), so we re-ran a converged core (L-BFGS on, three seeds).
The converged results, taken to two model scales with fair controls, give a
consistent negative. \textbf{(1) No quantum advantage:} at small scale the classical
PINN ($0.248$) beats every QAPINN; at resolved scale ($\sim$1273 params) the QAPINN
beats a \emph{shallow} classical ($0.094$ vs $0.24$, $p=0.003$) but is
statistically indistinguishable from both identically-trained fair controls (a
depth-matched classical $0.128$, $p=0.51$; a structural CFE $0.145$, $p=0.36$), so
the gain is architectural and
classically reproducible. \textbf{(2) No robust entanglement effect:} an $n=3$
``entanglement hurts'' signal ($p\approx0.02$) collapsed at $n=6$ ($p=0.41$) and is
retracted. We also correct the ``band $=$ depth'' framing (verified for the VQC
\emph{features}, but the tanh post-net escapes the band). The pipeline is
independently verified (8/8 checks) and every number matches its raw log; the
remaining roadmap (still-larger scale, more seeds, real CFD) is the subject of Sections
\ref{sec:roadmap}--\ref{sec:leftout}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)~\cite{raissi} make the differential
equation the loss. A Quantum-Assisted PINN (QAPINN)~\cite{qapinn} replaces the
first hidden layer with a variational quantum circuit (VQC), and is reported to cut
trainable parameters at maintained accuracy. The BQP challenge asks us to
\emph{explain} the learning dynamics --- when, why, and how the quantum layer
changes learning --- and to derive a problem-specific circuit-design methodology.

We adopt the dominant theoretical lens for VQCs (a data-encoding circuit is a
truncated Fourier series~\cite{schuld}) and test whether it suffices to explain a
QAPINN's behaviour, and how the quantum layer changes the classical network it is
attached to. Contributions and results mirror the submission report; the extended
value here is the roadmap (Sec.~\ref{sec:roadmap}) and scope boundaries
(Sec.~\ref{sec:leftout}).

\section{Background and Related Work}
A VQC with data-encoding gates is a partial Fourier series
$f(x)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}c_\omega e^{i\omega x}$: the frequency set $\Omega$ is
fixed by the encoding (with $L$ re-uploads, $\Omega=\{-L,\dots,L\}$), the
coefficients $c_\omega$ by the trainable gates~\cite{schuld}. Mhiri
et al.~\cite{mhiri} show the coefficients are additionally constrained and can
vanish with qubit count --- frequencies are necessary but not sufficient.
Differentiable quantum circuits solve DEs with analytic
derivatives~\cite{dqc}; hybrid QAPINN/QCPINN designs~\cite{qapinn,qcpinn} report
PDE-dependent parameter reduction (quantum sometimes \emph{loses}, e.g.\ cavity
flow); a ``black-hole'' collapse to the trivial solution is a distinct trainability
pathology~\cite{maxwell}. No validated mechanism explains \emph{when/why} the
quantum layer helps --- the gap targeted here.

\subsection{How the quantum layer changes the classical network}
A na\"ive comparison against a plain PINN suggested the quantum layer makes the
downstream neurons more diverse and drives larger gradients, but that comparison is
confounded (different post-net shape). We instead use a \emph{structurally
identical} classical-front-end control (CFE): the same post-net fed by
$\mathrm{Linear}(2\!\to\!w)+\tanh$ instead of the VQC. Against this fair control
(Table~\ref{tab:classical}), the effects do \emph{not} hold: accuracy is
bandwidth-gated (QAPINN ties at low depth/qubits, wins at depth 5 / 6 qubits /
heat); neuron diversity is inconsistent (classical front-end more diverse on the
depth axis, quantum more on qubits); and gradient amplification reverses at 5--6
qubits, where the QAPINN gradient variance shrinks with qubit count (a
barren-plateau signature). \emph{These single-seed readings are superseded by the
converged, multi-seed analysis (§\ref{sec:converged}):} the ``bandwidth-gated
advantage'' does not survive convergence and the ``barren-plateau signature'' is
withdrawn (wrong gradient measured, non-monotone trend). Nothing here survives as a
robust quantum-specific effect; the section documents only how the plain-PINN
comparison overstated the gap.

\subsection{Converged Re-analysis (Post-Audit)}\label{sec:converged}
The ablations above were run with L-BFGS \emph{disabled} (Adam 2000, 1500 points).
An adversarial audit established that this left every Burgers model undertrained
(rel-$L_2\approx0.42$--$0.57$, shock unresolved), so the ``differences'' were
movements inside a failed basin and, across six seeds, within initialisation noise.
\textbf{The results here supersede them.} We re-ran a converged core with L-BFGS
\emph{on} (Adam 2500 $+$ L-BFGS 400, 4000 points), three seeds each, on Burgers.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llcc l}
\toprule
config & model & params & rel-$L_2$ ($n{=}3$) & note \\ \midrule

cv\_qd5\_lin & QAPINN d5    & 137 & $0.314 \pm 0.073$ & depth helps; ties CFE \\
cv\_cfe      & CFE control  & 109 & $0.315 \pm 0.026$ & classical front-end \\
cv\_qd3\_none& QAPINN d3    & 121 & $0.369 \pm 0.022$ & \textbf{no-ent beats linear} \\
cv\_qd3\_lin & QAPINN d3    & 121 & $0.424 \pm 0.005$ & reference QAPINN \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Converged core. L-BFGS roughly halved every error vs.\ undertrained

\emph{Finding 1 --- no quantum advantage:} converged and matched, the classical PINN
($0.248$) beats every QAPINN; the best (depth-5, $0.314$) only reaches the CFE
control. \emph{Finding 2 --- larger-model + fair controls (the decider):} repeating
the core at $\sim$1273 params ($n=6$) against a structural control and a
depth-matched classical net (both trained under the QAPINN's exact protocol), the
QAPINN-none ($0.094$) beats a shallow classical ($0.241$, $p=0.003$) but is
statistically indistinguishable from a depth-matched classical ($0.128$, $p=0.51$)
and the CFE ($0.145$, $p=0.36$): no significant quantum advantage against a fair
baseline. (Under-powered: seed variance $\sim$0.08 $>$ the effect, and one anomalous
seed --- dropping it keeps the tie, $p=0.19$.) \emph{Finding 3 --- entanglement not robust:} the
$n=3$ ``entanglement hurts'' result ($0.369$ vs $0.424$, $p\approx0.02$) collapsed at
$n=6$ ($p=0.41$) and is retracted; at large scale the gap is likewise n.s.\ ($p=0.44$).
\emph{Framing correction:} ``band $=$ depth'' is verified for the VQC features
($L{=}1..6$) but the tanh post-net escapes the band (a depth-3 model reaches
$k{=}22$ once trained), so the earlier ``$k\approx3$ band edge'' was an undertraining
artifact; the ``barren-plateau in qubit count'' is withdrawn (wrong gradient
measured, non-monotone trend).

\subsection{Discussion}
With the converged core in hand the reading is one-directional: for this small-model
Burgers task the quantum layer offers \emph{no} accuracy advantage, and its one
clearly quantum-specific knob --- entanglement --- \emph{hurts} at convergence. The
``bandwidth'' story survives only weakly (more depth $=$ richer features, not a hard
model-band limit, and no advantage over a matched classical net). The value is
methodological and negative-with-attribution: verified pipeline, matched and
structurally-matched controls, multi-seed statistics, and an audit that caught our
own over-claims.

\section{Current Limitations}
The findings are firm in direction but bounded: (1) \textbf{modest $n$, underpowered
nulls} --- the decisive comparisons are \emph{failures to reject} (e.g.\ QAPINN-none
vs.\ depth-matched classical, $p=0.51$) under high seed variance; we caught one $n=3$
effect that reversed at $n=6$ (and which Welch's test never called significant even at
$n=3$, $p=0.06$), so all single-scale nulls are provisional; (2) \textbf{small-model
regime} --- at 121 params converged models sit at rel-$L_2$ $0.25$--$0.42$, so
orderings could shift with larger nets (we test one larger scale, not a full curve);
(3) \textbf{one PDE family, simulation only} (no shot noise / hardware); parameter
reduction is not resource reduction. The pre-convergence single-seed ablations are
retained for transparency but are superseded. Pipeline verified; every number matches
its log.

\section{Future Work and Experimental Roadmap}
\label{sec:roadmap}
Tiers 2a--2c below were the top priorities of the earlier draft; \textbf{they are now
done}, and their outcomes (Section~\ref{sec:converged}) are what reshaped the report.
We record them as completed, then give the genuinely open roadmap.

\subsection{Tier 2a --- Convergence analysis \textnormal{(\textbf{done})}}
Long-training the headline configs to $\sim$8000 iters, then the full converged core
(L-BFGS on), \emph{answered} the central worry: the undertrained orderings were
transients. Convergence roughly halved every error, and the QAPINN's apparent
accuracy advantage over a matched classical net \emph{disappeared}. A tentative
``entanglement harmful'' signal appeared here at $n=3$ but was later retracted (2b).

\subsection{Tier 2b --- Multi-seed robustness \textnormal{(\textbf{done})}}
Seeds proved decisive --- and cautionary. An $n=3$ ``entanglement hurts'' result
(none $0.369\pm0.022$ vs linear $0.424\pm0.005$, $p\approx0.02$) looked clean, but
expanding to $n=6$ \emph{collapsed} it ($0.379$ vs $0.402$, $p=0.41$); it was a lucky
three-seed draw and is retracted. This is the report's clearest lesson in
under-powered nulls, and it is why every single-scale comparison here is treated as
provisional pending more seeds.

\subsection{Tier 2c --- The classical-front-end control \textnormal{(\textbf{done})}}
The CFE (identical post-net, classical $2\!\to\!4$ front end) was built and run both
undertrained and converged. It \emph{refuted} the ``quantum diversifies the classical
neurons'' claim (diversity inconsistent across knobs; the earlier effect was a
mismatched-control artifact) and, converged, \emph{ties} the best QAPINN --- direct
evidence of no quantum advantage.

\subsection{Tier 2d --- Larger-model + mechanism \textnormal{(\textbf{done}; both deflated)}}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Resolved (larger-model) regime \textnormal{(done):}} we repeated the
  core at $\sim$1273 params ($n=6$) against a structural control (CFE) \emph{and} a
  depth-matched classical net. The QAPINN beats a shallow classical ($0.094$ vs
  $0.24$, $p=0.003$) but is statistically indistinguishable from the depth-matched classical ($0.128$, $p=0.51$) and CFE ($0.145$, $p=0.36$):
  the advantage is architectural, not quantum-specific. This is the decisive result.
  \item \textbf{Entanglement mechanism \textnormal{(done; moot):}} we measured feature
  correlation and effective rank for \texttt{cv\_qd3\_none} vs \texttt{cv\_qd3\_lin}.
  The metrics conflicted (correlation rose with entanglement but so did feature rank),
  and --- more importantly --- the entanglement \emph{effect} itself did not survive seed
  expansion (§Finding~3), so there was no robust effect left to explain.
  \item \textbf{Still open:} still-larger scale, $5$--$8$ seeds throughout, and a
  cross-PDE check --- none of which is expected to overturn the negative, but any could.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tier 3 --- Remaining ablation axes \textnormal{(largely done, single-seed)}}
The $\nu$-sweep, qubit, measurement, and encoding-scale sweeps were all run (undertrained,
single seed); their headline outcomes: \emph{$\nu$-sweep} --- error rises monotonically with
bandwidth but the advantage is V-shaped, \emph{not} a clean collapse, and the depth-3 QAPINN
loses to matched classical across the family; \emph{qubits} behave like depth; \emph{scale}
$=2\pi$ collapses (aliasing). These now need converged, multi-seed repeats to be trusted.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{$\nu$-sweep (bandwidth continuum, done):} tuning Burgers viscosity
  turned two regularity points into a curve; it \emph{refuted} bandwidth as a
  \emph{sufficient statistic} for advantage (V-shaped, not monotone).
  \item \textbf{Qubits} (3--6): more bandwidth + Hilbert space (changes params;
  needs re-matching + barren-plateau watch).
  \item \textbf{Measurement:} single / multi / ZZ, and the not-yet-implemented
  \emph{probability vector} readout that the challenge explicitly requests.
  \item \textbf{input\_scale} and \textbf{loss weighting:} secondary bandwidth /
  optimisation levers (the latter drove the earlier full-quantum collapse).
  \item \textbf{Encoding scheme:} the coordinate$\to$qubit map (round-robin vs
  split vs all-on-all).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tier 4 --- Real CFD and mathematical depth}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Lid-driven cavity (2-D incompressible Navier--Stokes):} the
  real-world CFD case the judges reward; the PDE-agnostic bench already supports
  multi-output, multi-equation systems. Literature suggests quantum may
  \emph{lose} here --- a valid ``what is lost'' finding.
  \item \textbf{Fourier coefficient reachability (Mhiri):} measure realised
  \emph{coefficients} vs the reference, not just the frequency support, to test
  whether the depth-5 bandwidth shortfall (max freq 4, not 5) is the coefficient
  constraint biting.
  \item \textbf{Barren-plateau / trainability study:} systematic gradient-variance
  vs qubits/depth over many random initialisations; relation to the
  ``black-hole'' collapse.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The deliverable --- from ``design methodology'' to a falsification protocol}
The challenge asks (Task~4) for a problem-specific circuit-design methodology. Our
data do not support one: with no robust quantum-specific advantage, a
proxy$\to$bandwidth$\to$circuit prescription would be premature. What our study
\emph{does} yield --- and what we offer as the transferable deliverable --- is a
\textbf{falsification protocol for claimed QAPINN advantages}, distilled from the
three over-claims it killed:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Train to convergence first} (L-BFGS, not Adam-only): most apparent
  advantages here were undertraining artifacts.
  \item \textbf{Compare against the right control:} a param-matched \emph{and}
  structurally-matched (same post-net) \emph{and} depth-matched classical net --- not
  an arbitrary MLP. Each stricter control killed a different apparent effect.
  \item \textbf{Require multi-seed significance} ($n\!\ge\!6$): an $n\!=\!3$ effect at
  $p\!=\!0.02$ here reversed at $n\!=\!6$.
  \item \textbf{Separate features from model:} verify claims about the VQC's Fourier
  band on the VQC output, not the post-net-filtered model output.
\end{enumerate}
Applied to our own results, this protocol converts three would-be positives into an
honest null --- which is precisely its value. A genuine design methodology becomes
warranted only once an advantage survives all four gates on some problem; the future
tiers below are how one would look for that.

\section{What Is Currently Left Out (Scope Boundaries)}
\label{sec:leftout}
For transparency, the present study explicitly does \emph{not} include (note: the
statistics/convergence/knob items of the earlier draft are now \emph{done} --- see the
converged and larger-model sections --- and are removed from this list):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Breadth of PDEs:} only Burgers and heat; no real CFD (lid-driven
  cavity, though \texttt{cavity.py} is built), and the $\nu$-continuum is single-seed.
  \item \textbf{Larger scales / more seeds:} the decisive comparisons are $n\!\le\!6$
  with high seed variance; still-larger models and $5$--$8$ seeds would firm the nulls.
  \item \textbf{Probability-vector readout:} the $\langle Z\rangle$/$\langle ZZ\rangle$
  measurements are ablated, but the probability-vector observable the challenge lists
  is not implemented (it breaks the higher-order autograd needed for $u_{xx}$).
  \item \textbf{Coefficient-level Fourier analysis:} we measure realised frequency
  \emph{support}, not the realised \emph{coefficients} vs their reachable set (Mhiri).
  \item \textbf{Hardware / noise:} statevector simulation only; no shot noise, no
  device runs; ``parameter reduction'' is not ``resource reduction''
  ($\sim$85$\times$ slower in simulation).
  \item \textbf{Validated design methodology:} because we find no robust
  quantum-specific advantage, a proxy$\to$bandwidth$\to$circuit prescription is not
  warranted by our data; what we offer instead (below) is a \emph{falsification
  protocol} for claimed advantages.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We have a verified bench, a five-dimension XAI suite, and --- crucially --- an
adversarial self-audit that repeatedly overturned our own readings. Across two model
scales, three control types (matched, structural, depth-matched), and multi-seed
statistics, the QAPINN offers \emph{no statistically significant} advantage over a
fairly-chosen classical baseline: at small scale classical wins, at resolved scale a
depth-matched classical net ties the QAPINN, and no robust entanglement effect
survives seed expansion. The ``VQC $=$ Fourier band $=$ depth'' fact is verified but
governs the quantum features, not the full model. The honest contribution is a
rigorous negative and the methodology behind it --- three apparent advantages each
dissolved under the appropriate fair control, and each would have been a false
positive without it. The open work is to test still-larger scale, more seeds, and
other PDEs before asking whether any quantum-specific advantage --- or design
methodology --- is warranted.
